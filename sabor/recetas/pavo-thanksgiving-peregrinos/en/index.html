Method

1

Thaw the turkey in the fridge: 1 day for every 4 or 5 pounds. Never on the counter.

2

Blend all the adobo ingredients.

3

Don't wash the turkey: pat it dry with paper towels.

4

With your fingers, loosen the breast skin and rub the adobo under the skin, inside and out.

5

Cover and refrigerate 12 to 24 hours.

6

Heat the oven to 325 °F (165 °C). Set the turkey on the rack, with the onion and broth in the pan.

7

Cover the breast with foil for the first half of the time. It's about 3 to 3 3/4 hours.

8

It's done when the thickest part of the thigh and breast reads 165 °F (74 °C).

9

Let it rest 20 to 30 minutes before carving.

THE TRICK

Go by the thermometer, not the clock: take it out at 165 °F (74 °C) and let it rest, so it doesn't dry out.

The story

Did the Pilgrims eat turkey in 1621? The only letter written about that feast, Edward Winslow's (published in London in 1622), mentions fowl… and 5 deer brought by Massasoit's Wampanoag men. We test 3 myths: whether there was turkey (William Bradford does mention wild turkeys that fall), whether it was really the first thanksgiving (St. Augustine, Florida, September 8, 1565: a mass and a shared meal with the Timucua, with chickpea cocido) and whether turkey has always been the dish (the idea starts in 1841 and Sarah Josepha Hale pushes it until Lincoln proclaims the holiday in 1863). Plus: what was NOT there in 1621 (potatoes, pumpkin pie, cranberry sauce) and 1939's "Franksgiving". At the end, a Latin adobo turkey that doesn't dry out, with the thermometer trick.

Kitchen note: check the ingredients for allergies or intolerances (egg, dairy, gluten, nuts, shellfish). Cook meat to a safe temperature (ground beef and pork 160 °F / 71 °C; poultry 165 °F / 74 °C). Amounts and times are a guide: every kitchen is different.
