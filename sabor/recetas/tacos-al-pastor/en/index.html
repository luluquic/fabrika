Tacos al Pastor at Home (No Trompo)

Method

1

Seed the guajillo and ancho chiles and soak them 15 minutes in hot water.

2

Blend the chiles with the achiote, pineapple juice, vinegar, garlic, onion, oregano, cumin, cloves and salt until smooth.

3

Coat the meat well with the adobo and refrigerate at least 4 hours (overnight is better).

4

Heat the comal until very hot. Cook the slices in batches, 2 or 3 minutes per side, until the edges are dark and crispy. Don't crowd the comal.

5

Chop the meat finely on the board and return it to the comal for 1 minute.

6

Sear a few pineapple slices on the same comal until caramelized and dice them.

7

Warm the tortillas two at a time and assemble: meat, pineapple, onion, cilantro, a squeeze of lime and your salsa.

THE TRICK

1) Very hot comal and a little meat at a time: if you crowd it, the meat releases juice and steams instead of browning. 2) The pineapple goes on the heat too: sear it on the same comal until caramelized before chopping.

The story

Mexico's most famous taco has a Lebanese passport. How lamb shawarma arrived by ship from Beirut (the first Lebanese immigrants reached Mexico in 1892), how in 1930s Puebla it became the taco árabe with pork, chipotle sauce and Arab bread (and why one of the pioneer families came from Iraq), where the name "al pastor" comes from (it already appears in an 1845 Mexican cooking dictionary), and how Mexico City turned it red, moved it to the corn tortilla and crowned it with pineapple (El Huequito, El Tizoncito, 1966). Then how to make tacos al pastor at home without a trompo, with 2 tricks.
