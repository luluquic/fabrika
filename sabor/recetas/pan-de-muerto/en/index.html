Method

1

Mix the flour, sugar, yeast and salt.

2

Add the eggs, warm milk, zest and orange blossom water, and knead about 10 minutes.

3

Work in the butter little by little and knead until the dough is smooth and elastic (it's sticky: don't add more flour).

4

Cover and let it rise until doubled, about 2 hours.

5

Set aside a piece for the bones and the little ball, and divide the rest into 4 round loaves.

6

Roll strips of dough with your fingers spread to form the bones; glue them in a cross with beaten egg and put the little ball in the center.

7

Let rise another 45 minutes.

8

Bake at 350 °F (180 °C) about 25 minutes, until golden.

9

Out of the oven, brush with melted butter and roll in sugar.

THE TRICK

1) The dough is sticky: don't add more flour, knead with lightly greased hands. 2) For the bones, roll the strip with your fingers spread and the knots form on their own.

The story

Pan de muerto wasn't born in Mexico… but it became the most Mexican bread of all. Why the famous legend of a heart coated in amaranth can't be true (wheat, cane sugar, milk and butter arrived with the Spanish), what was offered to the dead before wheat (the papalotlaxcalli, a tortilla stamped with a butterfly seal described by Friar Bernardino de Sahagún, and the huitlatamalli), how Castile's pan de ánimas (soul bread) reached New Spain (in Puebla and Tlaxcala some still call it that), what the bones, tears and little ball mean, and how it changes from Mixquic to Oaxaca. At the end, how to bake pan de muerto at home, with 2 tricks.
