Dry-Brined Turkey (Salt, Garlic, Oregano and Orange)

IngredientsMethodStory and video
ServesServes 10 to 11 (about a 12-pound turkey)

Ingredients

Turkey
Dry brine
Equipment

Method

1

Thaw the turkey in the fridge (one day for every 4 to 5 pounds). Don't wash it: pat it very dry with paper towels.

2

Mix the salt, garlic powder, oregano, cumin and orange zest.

3

Rub the mix inside the turkey and under the skin of the breast and thighs.

4

Set it on the rack in the pan, uncovered, in the fridge for 1 to 2 days. Don't rinse it.

5

Rub with 2 tablespoons of butter and roast at 325 °F (165 °C), with no stuffing inside, for about 3 hours or a little more.

6

Go by the thermometer: breast 165 °F (74 °C), thigh 170–175 °F (77–79 °C).

7

Let it rest about 45 minutes without covering it in foil, then carve.

THE TRICK

Dry salt 1 to 2 days ahead, no basting and no red button: the thermometer is the boss.

The story

Why does your Thanksgiving turkey come out dry? That little red button pops when the breast hits about 180°F… and turkey is already safe at 165°F. We test 3 myths: whether the button tells you when it's done, whether basting makes it juicier (America's Test Kitchen tested it with 3 turkey breasts) and whether you need to soak it in salt water overnight. And the final face-off: wet brine vs. dry brine. At the end, a dry-brined turkey with salt, garlic, oregano and orange, guided by a thermometer.

Kitchen note: check the ingredients for allergies or intolerances (egg, dairy, gluten, nuts, shellfish). Cook meat to a safe temperature (ground beef and pork 160 °F / 71 °C; poultry 165 °F / 74 °C). Amounts and times are a guide: every kitchen is different.
