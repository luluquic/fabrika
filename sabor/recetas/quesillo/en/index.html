Method

1

Put the sugar and water straight into the flan mold over medium heat. Don't stir with a spoon: just swirl the mold.

2

When it's the color of dark honey, turn off the heat and tilt the mold to coat the bottom and part of the sides. Careful, it burns. Let it harden.

3

Blend the condensed milk, milk, eggs and vanilla for a full minute (for the little holes). For a smooth one, just whisk lightly and let it rest 10 minutes.

4

Pour the mixture over the hardened caramel and close the mold tightly.

5

Water bath: set the mold in a pot with hot water halfway up, cover the pot and cook on low 50 minutes to 1 hour, without a hard boil. Or bake at 350 °F (180 °C) with water around it.

6

It's done when a toothpick comes out clean from the center.

7

Let it cool and refrigerate at least 4 hours (overnight is better).

8

Run a knife around the edge, put a deep plate on top and flip it in one move.

THE TRICK

1) The little holes come from air: blend a full minute; for a smooth quesillo, mix lightly and let it rest. 2) Water bath on low, no hard boil: if it boils, big holes form and it turns rubbery.

The story

It's called quesillo, "little cheese," and it doesn't have a single gram of cheese. And it isn't the first: the only complete cookbook surviving from ancient Rome (Apicius, De re coquinaria) has a "cheese patina" made with milk, eggs and honey, no cheese.
