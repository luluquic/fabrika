Homemade Churros with Hot Chocolate (No Churro Machine)

IngredientsMethodStory and video
ServesAbout 20 churros

Ingredients

Churros
Thick hot chocolate

Method

1

Bring the water to a boil with the salt.

2

Turn off the heat, add all the flour at once and stir with a wooden spoon until no dry flour remains.

3

Let the dough rest about 10 minutes and put it in a piping bag with a star tip.

4

Heat the oil to 350 °F (180 °C) in a deep pot (careful: it splatters).

5

Squeeze the bag and snip strips of about 5 in (12 cm) with scissors, right over the oil. Don't fry more than 4 at a time.

6

Fry 2 or 3 minutes, turning them, until golden. Drain on paper towels and roll in sugar.

7

Chocolate: heat the milk with the chocolate and sugar. Dissolve the cornstarch in a little cold milk, add it and stir over low heat about 5 minutes, until thick.

THE TRICK

1) Use a star tip: the ridges let steam escape so the churro doesn't burst. 2) Don't fry more than 4 at a time, so the oil doesn't cool down and they don't turn greasy.

The story

The churro you dip in chocolate has no known inventor, but it has three legends: that it came from China on Portuguese ships, that Castilian shepherds invented it after the horns of the churra sheep, and that it's Mexican. I test them against the sources: the Roman flour-and-water fritter, Chinese youtiao, the word "churro" in the Royal Spanish Academy dictionary (1884), the San Ginés inn in Madrid that became a churro shop in 1894, El Moro in Mexico City (1935) and how American cacao ended up next to the churro. Then homemade churros with no machine and thick Spanish hot chocolate, with 2 tricks.
