Homemade Tequeños (with US cheese)

Method

1

Cut the cheese into sticks 5/8 inch thick and about 2 3/4 inches long, pat them dry and keep them in the fridge.

2

Mix the flour with the salt, sugar and baking powder. Rub in the butter with your fingers.

3

Add the egg and then the water little by little until you have a soft dough that doesn't stick. Knead 5 minutes, cover and let it rest 20 minutes.

4

Roll the dough very thin (about 1/16 inch / 2 mm) and cut it into strips 5/8 inch wide.

5

Stick the end of a strip to one end of the cheese, cover that end well and wrap on a diagonal, overlapping each turn slightly, until you close the other end.

6

Freeze them 30 minutes before frying.

7

Fry in batches in oil at 350 °F (175 °C) for 2 to 3 minutes, until golden. Drain on paper towels.

8

In an air fryer: brush them with a little oil and cook at 390 °F (200 °C) for 8 to 10 minutes, turning halfway.

9

Serve hot, so the cheese stretches.

THE TRICK

1) A spiral with no gaps: cover both ends of the cheese well and overlap each turn. 2) 30 minutes in the freezer before frying, and oil at 350 °F (175 °C) measured with a thermometer.

The story

In 2023, Los Teques fried a 15-meter tequeño and took a Guinness World Record. But nobody can prove who made the first one. We test the 3 origin stories: the Báez sisters of Los Teques, the cook of a family vacationing in the mountains, and the Caracas neighborhood that no longer exists. Why the word "tequeño" is only recorded in the mid-1900s, what happened in 2023 with the Karol G controversy and the Cultural Heritage registry, and how the tequeño went from Venezuelan parties to a factory in Florida. Then how to make tequeños at home with cheese you can find in the US (frying cheese, halloumi or Latin white cheese), with 2 tips so they don't burst, deep-fried or in the air fryer.
