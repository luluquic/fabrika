Spaghetti with Beef and Ricotta Meatballs

The story

Is spaghetti and meatballs Italian? Between 1880 and 1920 about 4 million Italians came to the United States… and in Italy this dish barely exists. We test 3 myths: whether immigrants brought it from Italy (in Italy meat was a luxury; here, ground meat was cheap), whether Italians invented it (one of the first recipes is from London, 1861, and the first known US one was written by Juliet Corson in New York in 1888) and whether the 1955 movie made it famous (pasta makers were already advertising it in 1922). Plus: what real Italian polpette look like, and Teramo's chitarra with tiny meatballs. At the end, beef and ricotta meatballs that come out soft.

Method

Beef and ricotta meatballs (serves 4, about 16 meatballs): mix 1 lb ground beef, 1/2 cup whole-milk ricotta, 1 egg, 1/3 cup grated parmesan, 1/2 cup breadcrumbs, 2 minced garlic cloves, 2 tbsp parsley, 1 tsp salt and 1/2 tsp pepper; don't overmix. Roll golf-ball-size meatballs, brown them in olive oil and remove. In the same pot, cook 3 garlic cloves, add a 28-oz can of crushed tomatoes, salt and basil. Return the meatballs and simmer on low about 20 minutes, until 160°F inside. Cook 1 lb spaghetti in salted water and finish it 1 minute in the sauce.

Kitchen note: check the ingredients for allergies or intolerances (egg, dairy, gluten, nuts, shellfish). Cook meat to a safe temperature (ground beef and pork 160 °F / 71 °C; poultry 165 °F / 74 °C). Amounts and times are a guide: every kitchen is different.
