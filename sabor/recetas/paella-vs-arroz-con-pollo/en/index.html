Method

1

Season the chicken with salt, pepper and the cumin.

2

Brown it in the oil, about 4 minutes per side, and take it out.

3

In that same fat, sauté the onion and bell pepper 5 minutes; add the garlic, tomato and annatto.

4

Add the raw rice and stir 2 minutes, until it looks glossy.

5

Return the chicken to the pot, add the broth and beer, and taste for salt.

6

When it boils, turn the heat to the lowest setting, cover and cook 20 minutes without uncovering or stirring.

7

Scatter the peas on top, cover, turn off the heat and let it rest 10 minutes.

8

Check that the chicken is fully cooked (no pink near the bone) and serve.

THE TRICK

1) Toast the raw rice in the sofrito for 2 minutes: that keeps the grains separate. 2) Don't uncover or stir while it cooks.

The story

A single paella fed about 100,000 people in Valencia in 1992. But across most of Latin America the Sunday rice is a different one: arroz con pollo. Is one a copy of the other? We put them on 5 points: the origin (arroz a la valenciana printed in Mexico City in 1780, and "paella" used for the recipe in 1840), the pot (socarrat vs. pegao), the color (saffron, onoto, annatto and Peru's green arroz con pollo), the fame (Hemingway, the Guinness record and the 2021 cultural-heritage decree) and the table (the 10 ingredients of Valencian paella vs. a different arroz con pollo in every country).
