Original Fettuccine Alfredo (no cream)

Method

1

Take the butter out of the fridge ahead of time so it softens, and cut it into pieces.

2

Grate the parmesan very fine, almost like powder, and leave it at room temperature.

3

Boil water with a little salt and cook the pasta 1 minute less than the package says.

4

Before draining, save 1 cup of the cooking water.

5

Put the butter in a large skillet with the heat off, add the pasta and a splash of the water. Toss until the butter melts.

6

Add the cheese in 3 parts, off the heat, tossing quickly with tongs and adding water little by little until the sauce is creamy.

7

If it gets thick, add a bit more water. Serve right away, with black pepper if you like.

THE TRICK

1) Very finely grated parmesan at room temperature melts without lumps. 2) The cheese always goes in off the heat and in 3 parts, with the pasta water little by little.

The story

A restaurant in Rome sells more than 20 kilos of fettuccine Alfredo a day… without a drop of cream. We test 3 myths: that Alfredo di Lelio invented pasta with butter and cheese (there's a recipe from around 1460), that nobody in Italy knows it, and that Alfredo sauce has cream. The story of Ines and the "triple butter" pasta (1907–1908), Mary Pickford and Douglas Fairbanks's golden fork and spoon, the 1943 sale of the restaurant, and where the cream came from in the United States.
